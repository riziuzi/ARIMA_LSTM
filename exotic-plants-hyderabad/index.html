Where to farm: land vs transport

Land gets cheaper as you move away from Hyderabad, but every extra kilometre adds truck trips, plant damage and your own travel. The equation finds the distance where the total yearly cost is lowest.

The equation

Yearly cost of farming at a road distance of d km:

C(d) = Land(d) + Transport(d) + Visits(d) + Wages(d)

Land(d)      = (i − g) × A × P(d) × (1 − gap) × (1 + s)
P(d)         = Pf + (P0 − Pf) × e^(−d/λ)
Transport(d) = N × (c × d + toll + loader)
             + R × (δ0 + δ1 × d/100)
Visits(d)    = 24V × d × (car + τ ÷ speed)
Wages(d)     = L × D × W(d)

Setting the slope dC/dd to zero gives the best distance:

d* = λ × ln( K ÷ (λ × M) )

K = (i − g) × A × (P0 − Pf) × (1 − gap) × (1 + s)
M = N × c + R × δ1/100
  + 24V × (car + τ ÷ speed) + L × D × W′

K is the most land cost per year that moving away can save. M is what each extra km adds per year. Land cost falls fast near the city and slowly far out; transport rises steadily. The best point is where one more km saves as much land cost as it adds in transport. This is the classic land-rent trade-off von Thünen land-rent model.

What each loan type needs

  • Personal loan: no security. Fits the small start.
  • Loan against property: 9–11.5%, but you need property to pledge Loan against property rates.
  • Farmland purchase loan: for small and marginal farmers, tenants and sharecroppers SBI land loan (farmers) BoB land scheme.
  • Shade-net or polyhouse loan: up to 7 years with a 3–12 month break and a 10% margin, but the land is the security Central Bank poly/net house SBI Poly Loan.
  • Scheme-linked: NHB subsidy needs a bank term loan NHB schemes; the Agri Infra Fund pays 3% of the interest Agri Infra Fund; a Kisan Credit Card gives crop loans at 7% (4% if repaid on time) once land is in your name KCC / interest subvention.

Recommended: save for a year, then borrow ₹10–11 lakh

With ₹3 lakh saved, plan F needs a ₹15.4 lakh loan. The EMI is ₹34,700 at 12.5% over 5 years, leaving you ₹5,300 a month for anything that goes wrong.

Save ₹40,000 a month for 12 months instead (₹7.8 lakh in total). The loan drops to ₹10.6 lakh, the EMI to ₹23,900, and ₹16,100 a month stays free. Your salary can then carry the EMI through a weak first year, and from year 2 the farm’s cash covers the EMI.

Don’t borrow for land. ₹63 lakh is four times the loan your salary supports.

A sensible first plan

In this order:

  1. Keep your job. Save ₹40,000 a month for 12 months.
  2. Learn the trade meanwhile. Spend weekends at a Hyderabad nursery Kukatpally nursery hub, root cuttings at home, and line up 5–10 buyers: retail nurseries, landscapers, offices and online sellers.
  3. Lease 0.5–1 acre with a working borewell, 30–60 km out, on a registered lease of 5+ years Tenant survey: ₹14,936 avg Lease ₹25–35k with water.
  4. Before building, get the nursery licence and ask the Horticulture Officer whether your lease qualifies for the shade-net and drip subsidy. Wait for the sanction Nursery Rules 2017 MIDH guidelines.
  5. Build 1,000 m² of shade net (about ₹18 lakh with working money). Root your own cuttings and buy only mother plants.
  6. Borrow at most ₹10–11 lakh (EMI about ₹24,000).
  7. Grow to 2,000 m² only after six months of selling about 120 plants a day.
  8. Buy land after 2–3 profitable years, from farm profits, using real quotes in the calculator’s distance chart.
