Does growing exotic plants for Hyderabad pay?

Yes, as a well-run plant nursery on leased land where you root your own cuttings. Buying land does not pay back from farming yet. Every number links to its source, and the calculator lets you change any of them.

  • A nursery can pay if you control two things: the cost of each young plant and how many plants you sell. Rooting your own cuttings (about ₹30 a plant instead of ₹42) turns ₹1.8 lakh a year into ₹13.8 lakh. A 2,000 m² nursery has to sell about 250 plants every day of the year.
  • Don’t buy land yet. Lease 0.5–1 acre with water for ₹15,000–35,000 per acre a year [[lease]] [[lease2]]. One acre at 75 km costs about ₹63 lakh all-in. Buying it stretches the payback of the best plan from 1.8 to 4.2 years.
  • Borrow only what your salary alone can repay: about ₹10–15 lakh. That pays for the 1,000 m² start, not for land. Bank farmland loans are for farmers and tenants, not salaried buyers [[sbiLand]] [[bobLand]].
  • Polyhouse vegetables and dragon fruit don’t pay at today’s prices for an owner who must pay a supervisor. In this model capsicum needs about ₹110/kg all year to break even, against ₹40–300/kg in a 2025 case [[icarCapsicum]]. Dragon fruit has fallen to ₹40–70/kg [[dragonPrice]].
  • The rules are manageable: a nursery licence (₹1,000) [[nurseryRules]], at least ₹538 a day for workers [[minWage]], nil GST on live plants [[gst]], and little or no extra income tax at your salary (confirm with a CA).

Market and competition

India’s indoor-plant market is about US$610–660 million and grows about 7–8% a year [[marketSize]] [[marketSize2]]. Exotic vegetables are about US$2.7 billion, growing about 11% a year [[exoticVeg]].

In Hyderabad, Kukatpally has a cluster of nurseries [[kukatpally]], online sellers deliver plants across the city [[ugaoo]], and hotels and function halls were already buying exotic vegetables from local polyhouses in 2018 [[hotels2018]].

The main competitor is Kadiyam near Rajahmundry, over 400 km away by road. It has 600+ nurseries [[kadiyamScale]], sells small and medium plants at ₹25–100 [[kadiyamListing]], and ships to Hyderabad [[kadiyamFreight]]. To beat it, sell fresher stock, supply reliably and deliver fast inside the city.

Where to farm: land vs transport

Land gets cheaper as you move away from Hyderabad, but every extra kilometre adds truck trips, plant damage and your own travel. The equation finds the distance where the total yearly cost is lowest.

The equation

Yearly cost of farming at a road distance of d km:

C(d) = Land(d) + Transport(d) + Visits(d) + Wages(d)

Land(d)      = (i − g) × A × P(d) × (1 − gap) × (1 + s)
P(d)         = Pf + (P0 − Pf) × e^(−d/λ)
Transport(d) = N × (c × d + toll + loader)
             + R × (δ0 + δ1 × d/100)
Visits(d)    = 24V × d × (car + τ ÷ speed)
Wages(d)     = L × D × W(d)

Setting the slope dC/dd to zero gives the best distance:

d* = λ × ln( K ÷ (λ × M) )

K = (i − g) × A × (P0 − Pf) × (1 − gap) × (1 + s)
M = N × c + R × δ1/100
  + 24V × (car + τ ÷ speed) + L × D × W′

K is the most land cost per year that moving away can save. M is what each extra km adds per year. Land cost falls fast near the city and slowly far out; transport rises steadily. The best point is where one more km saves as much land cost as it adds in transport. This is the classic land-rent trade-off [[thunen]].

Labour

You keep your job, so someone reliable has to run the farm every day.

Daily wages

Near Hyderabad, farm and nursery workers earn ₹500–650 a day [[wageChevella]]. Farther out, routine work pays ₹325–475 [[wageSeries]]. Peak seasons push this up: Nalgonda paid ₹800 a day to women and ₹1,100 to men at transplanting in 2026 [[wageNalgonda]].

The legal minimum from 1 June 2026 is ₹538 a day (₹14,000 a month) for rural unskilled work [[minWage]] [[minWagePdf]]. The order is being challenged in the High Court [[minWageCourt]]; the older agriculture schedule was ₹396–468 [[wageAgri]]. The model pays ₹600 near the city and ₹540 far out, never below the floor.

A nursery needs about 1–1.5 workers per 1,000 m² [[oregon]]. The model uses 2 workers and a head gardener for 2,000 m², and 1 worker and a head gardener for 1,000 m².

Your take-home pay is ₹70,000 a month. Living costs of ₹30,000 are an example; set your own in the calculator. That leaves ₹40,000 a month.

Banks usually cap all EMIs at 40–50% of take-home pay [[foir]]. Unsecured personal loans run 10–16% for up to 5–6 years; 13–16% is realistic for a first loan [[plRealistic]] [[plRates]]. Rates are rising: RBI raised the repo rate to 5.50% on 7 Oct 2026 [[repo]] and banks lifted MCLR to about 8.9% [[mclr]].

What each loan type needs

  • Personal loan: no security. Fits the small start.
  • Loan against property: 9–11.5%, but you need property to pledge [[lap]].
  • Farmland purchase loan: for small and marginal farmers, tenants and sharecroppers [[sbiLand]] [[bobLand]].
  • Shade-net or polyhouse loan: up to 7 years with a 3–12 month break and a 10% margin, but the land is the security [[centPoly]] [[sbiPoly]].
  • Scheme-linked: NHB subsidy needs a bank term loan [[nhb]]; the Agri Infra Fund pays 3% of the interest [[aif]]; a Kisan Credit Card gives crop loans at 7% (4% if repaid on time) once land is in your name [[kcc]].

Recommended: save for a year, then borrow ₹10–11 lakh

With ₹3 lakh saved, plan F needs a ₹15.4 lakh loan. The EMI is ₹34,700 at 12.5% over 5 years, leaving you ₹5,300 a month for anything that goes wrong.

Save ₹40,000 a month for 12 months instead (₹7.8 lakh in total). The loan drops to ₹10.6 lakh, the EMI to ₹23,900, and ₹16,100 a month stays free. Your salary can then carry the EMI through a weak first year, and from year 2 the farm’s cash covers the EMI.

Don’t borrow for land. ₹63 lakh is four times the loan your salary supports.

A sensible first plan

In this order:

  1. Keep your job. Save ₹40,000 a month for 12 months.
  2. Learn the trade meanwhile. Spend weekends at a Hyderabad nursery [[kukatpally]], root cuttings at home, and line up 5–10 buyers: retail nurseries, landscapers, offices and online sellers.
  3. Lease 0.5–1 acre with a working borewell, 30–60 km out, on a registered lease of 5+ years [[lease]] [[lease2]].
  4. Before building, get the nursery licence and ask the Horticulture Officer whether your lease qualifies for the shade-net and drip subsidy. Wait for the sanction [[nurseryRules]] [[midh]].
  5. Build 1,000 m² of shade net (about ₹18 lakh with working money). Root your own cuttings and buy only mother plants.
  6. Borrow at most ₹10–11 lakh (EMI about ₹24,000).
  7. Grow to 2,000 m² only after six months of selling about 120 plants a day.
  8. Buy land after 2–3 profitable years, from farm profits, using real quotes in the calculator’s distance chart.
