Part 1 · The business · 4 min

Three businesses called “exotic plants”

Which kind of exotic-plant farm makes money near Hyderabad?

“Exotic plants” can mean three different businesses. Each sells a different thing to different buyers. The study ran all three on the same land, labour and transport numbers, so they can be compared fairly.

Indoor-plant nursery

You grow potted foliage plants such as Syngonium, Aglaonema and areca palm, and sell them to nurseries, landscapers and online sellers. A 6-inch plant sells wholesale for ₹40–150 Aglaonema ₹85–100 Areca palm ₹100. A batch is ready in about 6 months.

Polyhouse vegetables

You grow coloured capsicum, English cucumber or lettuce under plastic, and sell to hotels, supermarkets and traders. Coloured capsicum swings between ₹40 and ₹300 a kg ICAR capsicum case 2025.

Dragon fruit

You plant cactus vines on concrete poles and sell the fruit to traders. Full yield takes about 4 years. The price fell from ₹130–200 to ₹40–70 a kg as planting boomed TG farmer ₹130/kg (2025) Dragon fruit ₹40–70 (2026).

Worked out: break-even price against the market

The break-even price is the selling price at which sales just cover all running costs. If the market often pays less than that, the business loses money.

Market price rangePrice the model usesBreak-even price

Why the nursery wins

  • Money turns over fast. Two batches a year, and the first sales come within months.
  • You control the biggest cost. Rooting your own cuttings cuts the cost of each plant from about ₹42 to ₹30 (Lesson 2).
  • Plants wait. A potted plant keeps for weeks. Vegetables must be sold within days, so the buyer sets the price.
  • The polyhouse is costly to run. It needs a paid supervisor and an expensive structure. In this model capsicum needs about ₹109 a kg all year just to break even.

The market is real: India’s indoor-plant market is about US$610–660 million and grows about 7–8% a year India indoor plants market Ken Research estimate. The main competitor is Kadiyam near Rajahmundry, with 600+ nurseries selling small plants at ₹25–100 Kadiyam: 600+ nurseries Rajahmundry plants ₹25.

What I assumed, and why

  • Sizes: 2,000 m² for the nursery and the polyhouse, 2 acres for dragon fruit. These are small enough for one owner with a full-time job.
  • You keep your job, so the farm pays someone to run it daily: a head gardener at ₹15,000 a month for the nursery, a supervisor at ₹20,000 for the polyhouse Mali ₹13–15k Farm manager ₹20–30k.
  • All three are on leased land 40 km out (dragon fruit 75 km), so land cost doesn’t decide the comparison.

Check yourself

On leased land, which business did the model find clearly profitable?

The nursery makes about ₹13.8 lakh a year when you root your own cuttings. Polyhouse capsicum loses ₹3.4 lakh a year. Dragon fruit makes ₹1.7 lakh a year on ₹18 lakh invested, which takes more than 10 years to pay back.

Dragon fruit’s ₹70 a kg is above its ₹52 break-even. Why is it still risky?

Lots of new planting pushed prices down fast. At ₹70 it only just clears its costs, and only once the vines are mature. Another drop to ₹40–50 would wipe out the profit.

Remember

  • Compare businesses by their break-even price against the real market range.
  • The nursery is the one worth studying. The rest of the course follows it.

Land prices fall with distance

How much cheaper does farmland get as you move away from Hyderabad?

The research collected 59 asking prices and news reports for farmland in six directions around Hyderabad. Each dot below is one place. Prices fall fast over the first 50 km, then level off near ₹40 lakh an acre.

Worked out: the price curve

P(d) = Pf + (P0 − Pf) × e^(−d/λ)      ₹ lakh per acre

Pf      = ₹39 L    price far away, where it levels off
P0      = ₹931 L   where the curve starts at the city edge
P0 − Pf = ₹892 L   the city premium
λ       = 21 km    how fast the premium fades

The premium shrinks to about a third (e−1 = 0.37) every 21 km, so it halves every 0.69 × 21 ≈ 15 km. At 75 km:

e^(−75/21) = e^(−3.57) = 0.028
P(75) = 39 + 892 × 0.028
      = 39 + 25 = ₹64 lakh an acre (asking)

Part 2 · Where to farm · 5 min

Owning land still costs you every year

If you pay for land once, why does the model charge it as a yearly cost?

Money locked in land can’t earn anything else. If it came from a loan, you pay interest on it every year. If it came from savings, you give up the interest those savings would have earned. This is called the cost of money (or opportunity cost).

If land prices rise, part of that cost comes back when you sell. So the yearly cost of owning is the price × (cost of money − yearly price rise).

Part 2 · Where to farm · 7 min

The equation: finding the best distance

At what distance is land + transport + visits + wages lowest for the year?

Put Lessons 5–7 together. The yearly land cost falls fast near the city and slowly far away. Transport, visits and wages rise by about M for every km. Added up, they make a valley. The bottom of the valley is the best distance.

C(d) = Land(d) + Transport(d) + Visits(d) + Wages(d)

At the bottom, one more km saves exactly as much land cost as it adds in transport.

Worked out: from the idea to the formula

1. Land cost above the far-away floor:   K × e^(−d/λ)
   K = (i − g) × A × (P0 − Pf) × (1 − gap) × (1 + s)

2. One more km saves:   (K ÷ λ) × e^(−d/λ)
3. One more km adds:    M

4. Best where they are equal:
   (K ÷ λ) × e^(−d/λ) = M   →   e^(−d/λ) = λM ÷ K

5. Take the log of both sides:
   d* = λ × ln( K ÷ (λ × M) )
i − g
cost of money minus land price rise (9% − 0%)
A
acres bought (1)
P0 − Pf
city premium in the land curve (₹892 lakh)
gap, s
negotiated discount (10%); duties and broker (9%)
λ
how fast land prices fall (21 km)
M
what one more km adds a year (₹10,694, Lesson 7)

Worked out: with the research numbers

K       = 9% × 1 × ₹892 L × 0.90 × 1.09  = ₹78.8 lakh a year
λ × M   = 21 × ₹10,694                   = ₹2.25 lakh
K ÷ λM  = 78.8 ÷ 2.25                     = 35.1
ln 35.1 = 3.56
d*      = 21 × 3.56                       = 75 km

K is the most land cost per year that moving away can ever save. Because d* uses a logarithm, it moves slowly: doubling K only adds 21 × ln 2 ≈ 15 km.

Ten years of cash, and payback

When do you get your money back?

Each year’s cash = sales − running costs − any net replacement + the subsidy (year 1 only, if sanctioned). The running total starts at minus the money at start. Payback is when the running total crosses zero.

A new nursery starts slowly: 40% of full output in year 1, 80% in year 2 and 100% from year 3.

How much your salary can borrow

How big a loan can ₹70,000 a month carry safely?

A loan is repaid in equal monthly instalments, the EMI. Banks cap all your EMIs together at 40–50% of take-home pay. This cap is called FOIR, the fixed obligations to income ratio FOIR explained.

Worked out: the EMI formula, both ways

EMI = L × r × (1 + r)^n ÷ ((1 + r)^n − 1)

r = yearly rate ÷ 12 = 12.5% ÷ 12 = 1.042% a month
n = 5 years × 12 = 60 months

Turned around, the loan an EMI can carry:
L = EMI × (1 − (1 + r)^−n) ÷ r = EMI × 44.45

The rules that apply to you

Which Telangana and central rules apply, and what do they cost?

Verified means checked against an official, court or news page. Check means from a secondary source or knowledge as of mid-2026; confirm it before you rely on it.

Before you start

Nursery licenceVerified

Required. Apply to the Horticulture Officer. ₹1,000 for up to 4 lakh plants, valid 3 years. Keep records and use approved mother plants TG Nursery Act 2010 Nursery Rules 2017.

Buying farmlandVerified

A salaried non-farmer can buy farmland in Telangana. Avoid assigned land, land on the Section 22-A list, ceiling-surplus land and Scheduled (Agency) Areas. Register and mutate through Bhu Bharati and get a pattadar passbook Who can buy farmland (TG) Section 22-A list Bhu Bharati Act.

Land useCheck

Shade net, polyhouse and drip count as farming. A farmhouse, worker quarters or a shop needs NALA conversion and building permission NALA conversion.

Plant varieties and importsCheck

You may save, use, exchange and sell your own plant material, but don’t propagate protected varieties for sale without a licence Farmers' rights (PPV&FR). Imported plants need a permit and a phytosanitary certificate, so buy from Indian labs or importers Plant Quarantine Order.

Protected plantsCheck

Blue and Red Vanda, slipper orchids and Nepenthes khasiana need a Chief Wildlife Warden licence to grow or sell. Avoid them Specified plants (WPA 2022).

When you hire

Minimum wageCheck

₹538 a day (₹14,000 a month) for rural unskilled work from 1 June 2026; more for skilled work. The order is being challenged in the High Court, with no stay reported TG minimum wage Jun 2026 High Court case.

Labour codesVerified

In force from 21 Nov 2025. Pay on time (daily workers at the end of the shift, monthly staff by the 7th), equal pay, a weekly off, overtime at twice the rate. Injury cover from the first worker in horticulture. ESIC from 10 workers, EPF from 20 Labour Codes from 21 Nov 2025 Code on Wages s.17 Injury cover from worker 1 EPF 20+, ESIC 10+.

When you sell, and tax

GSTCheck

Nil on live plants, fresh fruit and vegetables. No registration is needed to sell your own produce. GST on pots, film and pesticides is a cost you can’t claim back CBIC GST rates GST Council.

PowerVerified

A horticulture nursery connection pays ₹4 a unit. Farm pumps get free power Power tariff (TGSPDCL).

Income taxCheck

Plants you grow yourself, nursery plants included, count as agricultural income. It isn’t taxed itself, but it is added to your salary to set your tax rate (“partial integration”) Income Tax Dept. Reselling bought-in plants is business income, so keep separate records.

My estimate with 2025–26 new-regime slabs and your ₹8.4 lakh salary: no extra tax while farm profit stays under about ₹9 lakh a year (plan F’s ₹5.4 lakh is fine), and about ₹21,000 a year at plan C’s ₹13.8 lakh. Confirm with a CA.

Support schemes

MIDH subsidyCheck

50% of the government cost norm for shade-net houses (₹710 per m²) and polyhouses, up to 4,000 m². Drip and misting: 55% for small farmers, 45% for others. Apply and get sanction before you build; a passbook is usually needed MIDH guidelines Micro-irrigation (PMKSY) TG Horticulture Dept.

Rythu BharosaVerified

₹12,000 per acre a year, only on land in your name where a crop is booked each season Rythu Bharosa ₹12,000/acre Only for cropped land (2026).

Not available to youVerified

PM-KISAN (land bought after 1 Feb 2019 doesn’t count), Rythu Bima (cover lapsed in Aug 2026) and PMFBY crop insurance (not running in Telangana) PM-KISAN rules Rythu Bima lapsed Crop insurance status.

Other schemesCheck

NHB pays 40–50% for larger bank-financed projects NHB schemes. Mudra loans up to ₹20 lakh suit a plant shop Mudra. PMEGP excludes horticulture PMEGP rules.
