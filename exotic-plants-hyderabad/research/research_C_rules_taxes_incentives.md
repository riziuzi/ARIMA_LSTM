# Research C: Land, farmers' rights, nursery rules, labour, taxes and incentives for an exotic-plant farm in Telangana

*Position as of 8 Nov 2026. Written for a 24-year-old salaried resident (₹70,000/month, about ₹8.4 lakh/yr) in Hyderabad who wants to buy farmland in Telangana, grow ornamental indoor plants (shade-net nursery), exotic vegetables (polyhouse) or dragon fruit, hire 3–10 workers and sell in Hyderabad.*

## Verification key (read this first)

- **✅ Verified:** checked in this session against the cited official, court, legal or news page.
- **🟡 Secondary only:** checked in this session, but only against a secondary or unofficial source (law-firm explainer, aggregator, blog). Probably right; confirm it.
- **⚠️ Not re-verified:** from the researcher's background knowledge (current to about mid-2026). It could not be re-checked in this session because the shared web-search budget ran out after about 46 searches. Direct page fetches were also blocked for news sites (telanganatoday.com, siasat.com). Each such item names the official site to check. Treat these as "probably correct, confirm before relying on them".
- **Coverage:** Sections 1–4 are mostly ✅ or 🟡. Most of Section 5 (income tax and GST details) and Section 6 (MIDH and NHB cost norms, AIF, KCC) are ⚠️ and need a follow-up check.

### Bottom line in plain English
1. **Buying land:** a salaried non-farmer can legally buy farmland in Telangana. Avoid land that is:
   - in a Scheduled (Agency) Area under the "1/70" rule;
   - assigned land;
   - on the Section 22-A prohibited list;
   - ceiling-surplus, Wakf, endowment or government land.

   Buy, register and mutate the land through the **Bhu Bharati** portal (Section 1).
2. **State and central benefits:**
   - **Rythu Bharosa** (₹12,000/acre/yr): you are likely eligible once the land is in your name and actually cropped.
   - **PM-KISAN:** you are **not** eligible (land bought after 1 Feb 2019).
   - **Rythu Bima:** lapsed in Aug 2026.
   - **PMFBY crop insurance:** not running in Telangana.
   - **Free farm power:** applies to ordinary agricultural pumps. A **horticulture-nursery** connection is billed at **₹4/unit** (Section 2).
3. **Licences:**
   - a **nursery licence** from the Horticulture Officer;
   - a **seed-dealer licence** if you sell vegetable seedlings;
   - a **plant-quarantine import permit** for imported plants;
   - **CITES** paperwork for CITES-listed species;
   - no Wildlife Act "specified plants" (e.g., Blue/Red Vanda, slipper orchids, *Nepenthes khasiana*) without a Chief Wildlife Warden licence (Section 3).
4. **Workers:**
   - pay at least the Telangana minimum wage, on time (from 1 Jun 2026: ₹14,000/month, about ₹538/day, for rural unskilled work);
   - you are liable for work injuries from worker no. 1;
   - ESIC, gratuity and maternity rules start at 10 workers; EPF at 20 (Section 4).
5. **Tax:**
   - Plants and produce you grow yourself, including nursery saplings and seedlings, are **agricultural income**. It is exempt, but it is added to your salary to set your tax rate. At your salary the tax still comes to **₹0** under the new regime (worked example, Section 5).
   - Reselling **bought-in** plants is taxable business income.
   - GST is **nil** on live plants, fresh flowers, fruit and vegetables.
6. **Subsidies:**
   - MIDH: about 50% on shade-net houses and polyhouses (up to 4,000 m²);
   - drip irrigation: 55%/45% central share, plus any state top-up;
   - NHB: 40–50% for larger bank-financed projects;
   - AIF: 3% interest subvention;
   - KCC: 7% interest (4% if repaid on time).

   **Apply and get sanction before you build** (Section 6).

## 1) Land rules: what it means for you

**Can a salaried non-farmer buy?**
- **Yes.** Telangana has no "agriculturists-only" purchase bar of the Karnataka/Maharashtra type. Any Indian citizen can buy, and the Bhu Bharati Rules 2025 let any person apply to register agricultural land.
  - Sources (🟡): https://2bigha.ai/blogs/agricultural-land-telangana-legal-rules ; https://1acre.in/guides/telangana/can-i-buy-land-in-telangana
  - The buyer carries all due-diligence responsibility.

**Land ceiling (A.P./Telangana Land Reforms (Ceiling on Agricultural Holdings) Act, 1973)**
- The limit is one "standard holding" per family unit (✅ https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/8693/1/act_1_of_1973.pdf):
  - Class A double-crop wet land: 10 acres;
  - Class A other wet land: 15 acres;
  - Class G dry land: 35 acres.
- Holdings of all family-unit members are added together, and 1 ha of wet land counts as 2.5 ha of dry land.
- Not a constraint for a small farm, but you may be asked for a ceiling self-declaration at registration (🟡 https://1acre.in/guides/telangana/can-i-buy-land-in-telangana).
- Land that was declared ceiling-surplus vested in the government and cannot be bought (🟡 https://assetlyhq.com/blog/land-ceiling-act-telangana-nadergul-explained/).

**Scheduled (Agency) Areas: the "1/70" rule**
- The A.P. Scheduled Areas Land Transfer Regulation 1959 was amended by Regulation 1 of 1970.
- In Scheduled Areas, any transfer of land to a non-tribal is **null and void**, including non-tribal-to-non-tribal sales. The land can pass only to tribals (or to government).
- Telangana areas covered: the Regulation was extended to parts of the then Adilabad, Warangal, Khammam and Mahabubnagar districts from 1-12-1963.
  - ✅ https://mcrhrdi.gov.in/2026/group1_batch2/week5/1.%20Costitutional%20provisions%20for%20protection%20of%20STs.pdf ; ✅ https://telanganatoday.com/rewind-whose-land-is-it-anyway-adivasis-land-rights-under-challenge ; ✅ https://csis.tshc.gov.in/hcorders/2007/wp/wp_21405_2007.pdf
  - Today these areas lie in districts such as Adilabad, Kumuram Bheem Asifabad, Bhadradri Kothagudem, Mulugu and parts of Nagarkurnool (⚠️ verify the village list).
- Non-tribals have challenged the regulation in the Supreme Court; no ruling found as of Nov 2026.
- **Practical rule: never buy in a notified agency village.** The usual belt around Hyderabad (Rangareddy, Medchal, Sangareddy, Siddipet, Yadadri, Vikarabad) is generally not Scheduled Area, but confirm with the Tahsildar (⚠️).

**Assigned lands**
- Land the government gave to landless poor families is covered by the Telangana (A.P.) Assigned Lands (Prohibition of Transfers) Act, 1977.
- It cannot be sold. A purchase is void, and the registering officer must refuse the document (Sec 5).
- ✅ https://www.latestlaws.com/judgements/telangana-high-court/2021/august/2021-latest-caselaw-2319-tel

**Section 22-A prohibited properties (Registration Act)**
- Sale, purchase or mortgage of a listed survey number cannot ordinarily be registered. The list covers:
  - government and poramboke land;
  - assigned land;
  - endowment (temple) land;
  - Wakf land;
  - ceiling-surplus land.
- Check the Prohibited Properties list on the Registration Department site (https://registration.telangana.gov.in, 🟡) and the "prohibited" flag in Bhu Bharati.
  - ✅ https://www.lkslaw.com/insights/articles/section-22-a-and-prohibited-properties-in-telangana-evolution-of-the-legal-framework ; ✅ https://www.thenewsminute.com/article/telangana-govt-prohibits-registration-waqf-properties-state-133868
- 2025–26 problems:
  - Thousands of acres of **private** land were wrongly put on the list (✅ https://theprint.in/india/telanganas-section-22a-mess-how-thousands-of-acres-of-private-property-landed-up-in-prohibited-list/3018683/).
  - The state then eased 22-A registration in GHMC, HMDA and DTCP areas (✅ https://www.thehansindia.com/telangana/relief-for-property-owners-22-a-restrictions-in-telangana-eased-1125337).
  - Being on the list does not prove the government owns the land. Listing must follow Rules 238–241 of the Telangana Registration Rules 2016 (🟡 lkslaw link above).
  - **Do not buy a 22-A-flagged parcel** even if the seller promises to have it removed.

**Telangana Bhu Bharati (Record of Rights in Land) Act, 2025: what changes for a buyer**
- Bhu Bharati replaced Dharani (ROR Act 2020). The Act and Rules took effect on **14 Apr 2025**, the same day the portal went live.
  - ✅ https://tshc.gov.in/documents/currentsection_2026_04_09T16_05_38.pdf ; 🟡 https://anantamias.com/bhu-bharati-telangana/
- **Buying:**
  - Search the land by survey number or passbook number.
  - Check the farmland encumbrance history on Bhu Bharati (it holds transactions from about 2018).
  - Book a slot. The sale is registered by the Tahsildar, who acts as joint sub-registrar for agricultural land.
  - Mutation into your name follows. You then get a **pattadar passbook**, which you need for Rythu Bharosa, a KCC loan, a farm power connection and subsidies.
  - 🟡 https://anantamias.com/bhu-bharati-telangana/ ; 🟡 https://www.landeed.com/post/dharani-to-bhu-bharati---a-strategic-shift
- **Survey map and Bhudhaar ID:**
  - The Act provides for a survey sketch or map with registration and mutation, and for a unique land-parcel ID ("Bhudhaar").
  - It is rolled out in phases (⚠️ not re-verified). Insist on a demarcation survey before paying.
- **Disputes and appeals:**
  - First appeal under Sec 15: within about 60 days, to the RDO or Collector.
  - Second appeal to the **Land Tribunal** (Sec 14): within about 30 days. Until a tribunal is set up, the Commissioner (CCLA) acts as the Tribunal.
  - The High Court has held that factual land disputes go to the Tribunal, not to a writ court, and that mutation orders must give reasons.
  - ✅ https://tshc.gov.in/documents/currentsection_2026_07_13T11_47_42.pdf ; 🟡 https://assetlyhq.com/blog/bhu-bharati-appeal-land-tribunal-telangana-guide/ ; ✅ https://www.latestlaws.com/judgements/telangana-high-court/2025/june/2025-latest-caselaw-3997-tel

**Conversion (NALA) and building permissions**
- **Conversion law:** non-agricultural use needs conversion under the Telangana Agricultural Land (Conversion for Non-Agricultural Purposes) Act, 2006 ("NALA").
  - Applications go through Bhu Bharati's NALA module. The Tahsildar or RDO decides, and the portal auto-calculates the conversion charge.
  - Prohibited-list land cannot be converted.
  - 🟡 https://unadhi.com/property/nala-agricultural-land-conversion-non-agricultural-hyderabad-telangana-bhu-bharati.html ; 🟡 https://cclatsdharani.com/nala-conversion-telangana-process/
- **Shade-net house, polyhouse, drip system, borewell, pump room:**
  - These are agricultural use (protected cultivation). The state horticulture department has itself subsidised polyhouses on farmland (https://horticulture.tg.nic.in/PolyHouse/PolyHouse.html, ✅ page exists).
  - **No official Telangana rule was found that either requires or exempts** NALA or building permission for them. Confirm in writing with the Tahsildar and the Gram Panchayat secretary, especially for large steel greenhouses.
- **Farmhouse or workers' quarters (residential), office, shop or packing shed:**
  - Treat these as non-agricultural.
  - Expect NALA conversion of the footprint, then building permission from the Gram Panchayat. Inside urban development areas, the permission comes from HMDA/DTCP or TG-bPASS.
  - 🟡 https://1acre.in/blog/construction-in-farmland-in-telangana. Small store or tool sheds are a grey area; ask locally.

## 2) Farmers' rights and farmer benefits

**Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers' Rights Act, 2001 (PPV&FR)**
- **Farmers' privilege (Sec 39(1)(iv)):** you may save, use, sow, re-sow, exchange, share or sell your farm produce, including seed of a protected variety, **but not as "branded seed"**.
  - ✅ https://vikaspedia.in/agriculture/policies-and-schemes/crops-related/protection-of-plant-varieties-and-rights-of-farmers/farmers2019-rights-in-the-ppv-fr-act-2001
- **Other rights:**
  - registering farmers' own varieties;
  - compensation if registered seed fails to perform as claimed (Sec 39(2));
  - protection for innocent infringement (⚠️ Sec 42).
- **Breeders' rights:** the breeder of a registered variety has exclusive rights to produce, sell, market, import and export it (Sec 28). The farmers' privilege is **not a blanket licence for a commercial nursery**.
  - Mass-propagating and selling planting material of a **registered (protected) ornamental variety** as a nursery business needs the breeder's authorisation (licence or royalty).
  - 🟡 https://ksandk.com/intellectual-property-rights/farmers-rights-ppvfr-act-section-39-privilege/
- **Patents:** plants and plant varieties **cannot be patented in India** (Patents Act Sec 3(j)) (🟡 https://www.asiaiplaw.com/index.php/section/ip-analysts/analysis-of-section-3j-of-the-patents-act-1970).
  - Imported branded ornamentals often carry foreign plant-breeder rights or "propagation prohibited" tags, and supplier contracts can still bind you.
  - Check the PPVFRA register before propagating a named variety (⚠️ https://plantauthority.gov.in).

**Rythu Bharosa (Telangana)**

  - No acreage cap: an earlier cap proposal was dropped (🟡 https://anantamias.com/rythu-bharosa/).

## 3) Plant and nursery regulation

**Nursery licence (mandatory)**
- **The law:** Telangana Registration of Horticulture Nurseries (Regulation) Act, 2010 (Act 13 of 2010) and Rules 2017. Every horticulture nursery owner must hold a licence from the competent authority.
  - ✅ https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/8086/1/act_13_of_2010.pdf ; ✅ https://horticulture.tg.nic.in/Downloads/ACT/Nursery%20act%202017.pdf ; ✅ https://horticulture.tg.nic.in/Act.html ; 🟡 https://www.thehansindia.com/telangana/telangana-nursery-act-should-be-national-model-says-sklthu-vc-1030490
- **How to apply:** to the **Horticulture Officer** for your area. The licence is **valid 3 years**; apply for renewal at least 30 days before expiry.
- **Fee (2017 Rules):** ₹1,000 for production up to 4 lakh plants; ₹2,500 above that.
- **Ongoing duties:**
  - display the licence;
  - keep records and a copy of the Act and Rules;
  - keep the nursery sanitary;
  - use approved mother plants.
- **Enforcement:** officers can inspect, suspend or cancel the licence, and there are appeal and revision routes. Penalty amounts were not found.
- **Ornamental nurseries:** the exact definition of "horticulture nursery" was not checked. Assume ornamental plant nurseries are covered and confirm with the Horticulture Officer.

**Seeds Act 1966 and Seeds (Control) Order 1983**
- **What counts as "seed":** seeds of food crops (including fruits and vegetables), cotton, fodder and jute. It **includes seedlings, tubers, bulbs, rhizomes, roots, cuttings, grafts and other vegetatively propagated material** of food crops or fodder.
- **Licence:** selling "seeds" needs a dealer licence under the 1983 Order, valid 3 years.
- **Inference (not an official ruling):**
  - selling **vegetable seedlings** probably needs a seed-dealer licence from the state Agriculture Department;
  - **ornamental plants are outside the Seeds Act**;
  - growing for your own use needs no licence.
- ✅ https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/13686/1/the_seeds_act%2c_1966.pdf ; ✅ https://py.gov.in/sites/default/files/seedcontrolorder1983.pdf
- **Draft Seeds Bill 2025:** would replace the Act and Order, with mandatory registration of varieties, dealers and producers and QR codes. As of Nov 2026, **no evidence it has been enacted**.
  - ✅ https://www.icar.org.in/sites/default/files/Circulars/SeedsBill_12112025.pdf ; 🟡 https://india.mongabay.com/2026/01/indias-long-search-for-a-fair-seed-law-remains-unfinished-commentary/ ; 🟡 https://www.pressreader.com/india/businessline-hyderabad-9wvx/20260112/282033333581692

**Pesticides (Insecticides Act 1968)**
- ⚠️ Not re-verified.
- Use only products **registered by CIB&RC** with a **label claim for your crop**, bought from a licensed dealer with a bill.
- Bio-pesticides also need registration.
- Banned and restricted lists apply. For example, glyphosate is restricted to pest-control operators under a 2022 central order.
- A farmer using pesticides needs no licence; selling them does.
- The Pesticide Management Bill 2020 had not been enacted as last known.
- Portal: https://ppqs.gov.in (✅ domain).

**Importing exotic plants or tissue-culture material (Plant Quarantine (Regulation of Import into India) Order, 2003, as amended)**
- **Permit:** no plant or planting-material consignment may enter India without a valid import permit.
  - Apply on **Form PQ 02** (seeds and plants for propagation) **at least a month in advance**.
  - Each species falls under one of Schedules V, VI or VII, with specific conditions.
- **Paperwork and inspection:**
  - an official **phytosanitary certificate** from the exporting country;
  - inspection and fees at the Plant Quarantine Station;
  - fumigation or treatment if pests are found.
- **Fees:** the 2003 text listed ₹300 per permit for propagation material (₹150 for consumption items); later amendments may have changed this.
- **Tissue culture:** virus-free tissue-culture plants under Schedules V/VI are exempt from the permit under clause 10(3) but are still inspected.
- **Post-entry quarantine:** applies to specified material in approved facilities, e.g., banana for 9–12 months.
- **Before importing:** check the species' schedule at https://plantquarantineindia.nic.in (PQIS).
- ✅ https://upload.indiacode.nic.in/showfile?actid=AC_CEN_23_31_00008_191402_1523342057238&filename=0-pq-order-1322%28e%29-2003.pdf&type=notification ; ✅ https://indiankanoon.org/doc/166301049/ ; ✅ https://plantquarantineindia.nic.in/pqispub/docfiles/Schedule-V.htm ; ✅ https://ibkp.dbtindia.gov.in/DBT_Content_Test/CMS/Guidelines/20181115121925276_PlantQuarantine_order_2003.pdf
- **Practical point:** buying from Indian tissue-culture labs or importers who already hold permits avoids most of this.

**Wildlife (Protection) Act 1972 as amended in 2022, and CITES**
- **Schedule III "specified plants":** you need a licence from the **Chief Wild Life Warden** to **cultivate** a specified plant (Sec 17C) or to **deal** in it (Sec 17D), and stocks must be declared.
  - The list includes Beddome's cycad, **Blue Vanda and Red Vanda orchids, Ladies' slipper orchids (*Paphiopedilum* spp.), Pitcher plant (*Nepenthes khasiana*)**, Kuth and others.
  - 🟡 https://www.drishtiias.com/daily-updates/daily-news-analysis/wildlife-protection-amendment-bill-2021 ; 🟡 https://vajiramandravi.com/current-affairs/wildlife-protection-act/ ; act text: https://faolex.fao.org/docs/pdf/IND021932.pdf
- **Schedule IV (CITES species):**
  - The Sec 49M registration rules notified so far (2024) cover **living animals only**. No plant-registration rule was found.
  - Import or export of CITES-listed plants still needs CITES permits. Under CITES, artificially propagated specimens are treated more leniently, but paperwork is still needed across borders.
  - CITES-listed plant groups (⚠️): all orchids, cacti, *Nepenthes*, cycads, many succulent *Euphorbia* and *Aloe* species.
  - 🟡 https://pwonlyias.com/current-affairs/living-animal-species-rules-2024/ ; 🟡 https://scholarworks.uni.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=1004&context=awards_bolton
- **For you:** stock hybrids and artificially propagated plants from documented sources. Avoid wild-collected native orchids, *Nepenthes khasiana* and cycads unless licensed.

**Biological Diversity Act 2002 (amended 2023, in force 1 Apr 2024)**
- Growers and cultivators are exempt from the prior-intimation requirement.
- Cultivated medicinal plants are exempt if you hold a certificate of origin from the local Biodiversity Management Committee.
- Indian entities only intimate the State Biodiversity Board when using bio-resources commercially (e.g., selling raw material to industry).
- For an ornamental or vegetable farm: **normally no action needed**.
- 🟡 https://www.mondaq.com/india/life-sciences-biotechnology-nanotechnology/1350780/the-biological-diversity-amendment-bill-2023-an-overview ; 🟡 https://lexplosion.in/the-biological-diversity-amendment-act-2023-to-be-effective-from-1st-april-2024/

**Invasive or banned species**
- Not researched in this session (gap).
- ⚠️ Gujarat banned new planting of *Conocarpus* in 2023. Telangana's position was not verified, so avoid it as a precaution.

## 4) Labour rights and your obligations as an employer

**Legal status of the Labour Codes**
- The **four Labour Codes** came into force on **21 Nov 2025** and replaced 29 central laws: Code on Wages 2019, Industrial Relations Code 2020, Code on Social Security 2020, and Occupational Safety, Health & Working Conditions (OSH) Code 2020.
- Central rules followed. Some state rules and provisions were phased.
- ✅ https://www.ey.com/content/dam/ey-unified-site/ey-com/en-in/alerts-hub/2025/11/new-labour-codes-implemented-across-the-country-effective-21-november-2025.pdf ; ✅ https://ksandk.com/labour-employment/all-four-labour-codes-enforced-from-21-november-2025/ ; 🟡 https://taxguru.in/corporate-law/major-labour-code-sections-force-notifications.html ; 🟡 https://employmentlaw.lkslaw.com/what-changed-2025

**Minimum wage (Code on Wages)**
- **The June 2026 order:** Telangana's **G.O.Ms.No.6 (30 May 2026; effective 1 Jun 2026)** revised wages for all employments under the Code on Wages, in 3 zones with 4 skill levels.
  - Zone I = municipal corporations; Zone II = municipalities; Zone III = rural and other areas.
  - **Zone III unskilled: ₹14,000/month = ₹538.46/day** (monthly ÷ 26).
  - Zone I runs from ₹16,000 (unskilled) to ₹20,000 (highly skilled).
  - VDA is ₹33.86 per CPI point per month above 443.
  - The order is under challenge in the High Court (hearing adjourned to 20 Aug 2026); no stay reported.
  - 🟡 https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/56455/telangana-govt-issued-the-revision-of-minimum-wages-across-all-employm/ ; 🟡 https://unitedconsultancy.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Telangana-New-Minimum-Wages-GO-No-6-Date-30-May-2026.pdf ; ✅ https://www.siasat.com/hc-gives-telangana-final-chance-to-explain-minimum-wage-delay-3518887/ ; 🟡 https://labourcodesadvisor.com/minimum-wages/telangana/
- **The older agriculture-specific schedule** (VDA period Apr–Sep 2026; may be superseded):
  - Zone II ploughing ₹396/day;
  - Zone I ploughing ₹468/day;
  - Zone I sowing/transplanting/harvesting ₹457/day.
  - A separate **horticulture** schedule set "Mali/Gardener" at ₹14,136/month.
  - 🟡 https://wageindicator.org/en-in/work-in-india/minimum-wage/21802-telangana/36292-agriculture ; 🟡 https://wageindicator.org/en-in/work-in-india/minimum-wage/21802-telangana/36939-horticulture
- **Safe rule:** pay **at least ₹538/day (₹14,000/month) for rural unskilled workers** and more for skilled ones (e.g., grafting or polyhouse technicians).
- **National floor wage:** ₹178/day (2019 figure); state rates are well above it (🟡 https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/floor-wage-section-9-code-on-wages-vs-minimum-wage/).

**Other Code on Wages duties (apply to every employee, including farm workers)**
- **Pay on time (Sec 17):**
  - daily-rated: at the end of the shift;
  - monthly: before the 7th of the next month;
  - weekly: on the last working day of the week (⚠️);
  - fortnightly: within 2 days after the fortnight (⚠️);
  - on termination or resignation: within 2 working days.
  - ✅ https://www.advocatekhoj.com/library/bareacts/codeonwages/17.php ; https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/15793/1/aA2019-29.pdf
- **No gender discrimination** in pay or recruitment (Sec 3) (✅ same Act).
- **Hours and overtime:**
  - normal working day as notified (generally 8 hours, ⚠️);
  - one rest day in every 7;
  - overtime at **at least twice** the normal rate (Secs 13–14, ⚠️).
- **Deductions:** only authorised deductions; keep wage and attendance registers (⚠️).
- **Bonus:** applies only at 20+ employees (⚠️).

**Social security (Code on Social Security 2020)**
- **Employees' compensation (injury or death at work):** applies **from the first worker** in the listed employments, which include "employed in **horticultural operations**, forestry, bee-keeping or farming by tractors or other power-driven machinery".
  - **Take an Employees' Compensation (workmen's comp) insurance policy** (⚠️ product not researched).
  - 🟡 https://bareactschedules.blogspot.com/2020/10/schedule-2-of-code-on-social-security.html
- **ESIC:** establishments with **10+ employees**; voluntary below 10 (🟡). Farm operations have historically been outside ESI notification; check with ESIC (⚠️ https://www.esic.gov.in).
- **EPF:** establishments with **20+ employees** (✅ https://www.mondaq.com/india/employee-benefits-compensation/1735668/part-1-of-indias-new-labour-codes-code-on-social-security-2020).
- **Gratuity:** 10+ employees. Due after 5 years (or on death or disablement). Fixed-term staff get it pro-rata, generally after 1 year.
  - 🟡 https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-gratuity-under-code-on-social-security/ ; 🟡 https://bhattandjoshiassociates.com/the-gratuity-conundrum-for-fixed-term-employees/
- **Maternity benefit:** 26 weeks' paid leave in covered establishments (factories, plantations, and shops/establishments with 10+ employees) (⚠️).
- **Gig and unorganised workers:** encourage workers to register on **e-Shram** (https://eshram.gov.in, ⚠️).

**OSH Code and other laws**
- **Inter-State migrant workers:** the OSH Code's migrant-worker chapter applies when **10+ inter-State migrant workers** were employed on any day in the past 12 months. It covers both contractor-recruited workers and those who came on their own.
  - Duties include an annual lump-sum return-journey fare, records and portal reporting, and decent working conditions.
  - ✅ https://ksandk.com/labour-employment/inter-state-migrant-workers-osh-code-2020/
- **Registration and appointment letters:** registration and appointment letters under the OSH Code apply to "establishments" with 10+ workers. Issue simple **written appointment letters** to everyone anyway (⚠️).
- **Child labour:** Child & Adolescent Labour (Prohibition & Regulation) Act 1986 (⚠️ not re-verified):
  - no child under 14 may work, except helping in a family enterprise outside school hours;
  - no one under 18 in hazardous work. **Do not let under-18s spray pesticides.**
- **Bonded labour:** wage-advance-linked bonded labour is illegal (⚠️).
- **Sexual harassment (POSH Act):** Internal Committee needed at 10+ employees; below that, complaints go to the district Local Committee (⚠️).
- **Shops & Establishments registration:** needed if you open a **retail plant shop or office in Hyderabad**, under the Telangana Shops & Establishments Act (⚠️ https://labour.telangana.gov.in).

**Plain-language employer checklist (3–10 workers)**

| Headcount | What applies |
|---|---|
| 1+ worker | Minimum wage (≥₹538/day rural unskilled); pay on time; equal pay; weekly off; overtime ×2; wage/attendance register; employees' compensation liability (insure); no child labour; no pesticide spraying by under-18s; written appointment letter. |

## 5) Taxes

> ⚠️ Most items in this section are **not re-verified in this session** because the search budget ran out. They reflect the law as last known (mid-2026). Have a CA confirm before filing.

**Income tax on farm and nursery income**
- **The exemption:** agricultural income is **exempt** (Income-tax Act 1961, Sec 10(1)), as defined in Sec 2(1A). **Explanation 3** (since AY 2009-10) says income from **saplings or seedlings grown in a nursery** is agricultural income. So potted ornamental plants you raise yourself qualify, even without "basic operations" on land. (https://incometaxindia.gov.in)
- **New Act:** the **Income-tax Act 2025** (assented Aug 2025, in force **1 Apr 2026**) keeps the definition, including the nursery deeming, and the exemption (Sec 11 with Schedule II). It renames "previous year/assessment year" as "tax year". Confirm the section numbers.
- **What counts as agricultural income:**
  - dragon fruit, vegetables and flowers you grow;
  - ornamental plants you propagate and grow on.
- **What is business income (taxable at slab rates):**
  - **buying finished plants and reselling them** (trading);
  - pots, planters, potting mixes and landscaping services.
  - Presumptive taxation may be available for small turnover (Sec 44AD of the 1961 Act and its equivalent in the 2025 Act).
  - If you buy plugs or tissue-culture plantlets and grow them on for months, that is closer to cultivation. Keep separate stock records and books for "grown" and "bought-for-resale" items.
- **Partial integration:** applies if agricultural income is over ₹5,000 **and** other income is over the basic exemption (₹4 lakh, new regime). Agricultural income is then used only to push your other income into a higher slab.
- **Filing:** with more than ₹5,000 of agricultural income you cannot use ITR-1. Use ITR-2, or ITR-3 if you also have trading income. Keep land papers, sale bills and wage records; large agricultural-income claims get scrutinised.

**Worked example: FY 2026-27 (tax year 2026-27), new regime**

Assumptions: slabs and rebate the same as FY 2025-26:
- slabs: 0–4L nil; 4–8L 5%; 8–12L 10%; 12–16L 15%; 16–20L 20%; 20–24L 25%; >24L 30%;
- standard deduction ₹75,000;
- s.87A rebate up to ₹60,000 if total income ≤ ₹12 lakh;
- cess 4%.

| Step | Amount (₹) |
|---|---|
| Gross salary | 8,40,000 |
| Less standard deduction | 75,000 |
| **Non-agricultural (total) income** | **7,65,000** |
| Net agricultural income (exempt but aggregated) | 3,00,000 |
| A. Tax on 7,65,000 + 3,00,000 = 10,65,000 → (4L×5%=20,000) + (2.65L×10%=26,500) | 46,500 |
| B. Tax on 4,00,000 (basic exemption) + 3,00,000 = 7,00,000 → 3L×5% | 15,000 |
| Tax after aggregation (A − B) | 31,500 |
| Rebate s.87A (total income 7.65L ≤ 12L) | −31,500 |
| **Tax payable (incl. 4% cess)** | **0** |

What the example shows:
- **At your current salary the farm income costs no extra tax.** Without the farm income, tax would also be nil.
- **When the classification matters (trading vs. own-grown):** consider salary ₹10 lakh (taxable ₹9.25L) plus ₹3L of profit.
  - If the ₹3L is agricultural: tax is **nil**. After aggregation it is ₹48,750, fully covered by the rebate.
  - If the ₹3L is **trading** profit: total income is ₹12.25L, so there is no full rebate. Marginal relief caps the tax at ₹25,000 + cess = **₹26,000**.
- **Higher salary later:** taxable salary ₹15L plus ₹3L agricultural income.
  - Tax = tax on ₹18L (₹1,60,000) − tax on ₹7L (₹15,000) = ₹1,45,000 + cess = **₹1,50,800**.
  - With no farm income, tax would be ₹1,09,200. Farm income adds about ₹41,600 by pushing salary into higher slabs, even though it is "exempt".
- The rebate interaction above is the standard reading (rebate applies to tax on "total income", which excludes agricultural income). Have a CA confirm.

**Capital gains when you sell the land**
- **Rural agricultural land is not a "capital asset"**, so there is **no capital-gains tax**.
- **"Urban" agricultural land:**
  - inside a municipality or cantonment with population ≥10,000; or
  - within these aerial distances of such a town: ≤2 km (population 10,001–1 lakh), ≤6 km (1–10 lakh), ≤8 km (>10 lakh).
- **Urban land is taxed:**
  - LTCG (held more than 24 months) at **12.5% without indexation** for transfers on or after 23 Jul 2024;
  - otherwise STCG at slab rates;
  - **Sec 54B** relief if the land was used for agriculture for 2 years and you buy other agricultural land within 2 years.
- **TDS on purchase:** 1% TDS on buying property worth ₹50 lakh+ (Sec 194-IA) does **not** apply to rural agricultural land.
- Source: https://incometaxindia.gov.in

**Land revenue and property tax**
- Not verified. As far as known, Telangana collects **no annual land revenue on agricultural land**.
- A Gram Panchayat can levy **house/property tax on buildings** (e.g., a farmhouse) under the Telangana Panchayat Raj Act 2018 (⚠️).
- Confirm with the Tahsildar and the Panchayat secretary.

**GST**
- **Rates on your output (⚠️ high confidence):**
  - **Nil** on live plants, cuttings, bulbs and roots (HSN 0601/0602);
  - nil on **fresh** cut flowers and foliage (0603/0604);
  - nil on **fresh** vegetables (07) and **fresh** fruit, including dragon fruit (08).
  - Dried or dyed flowers, frozen or dried produce, and branded pre-packaged items can be taxable.
  - Exemption notification 2/2017-CT(Rate), as amended; check at https://cbic-gst.gov.in.
- **Registration:**
  - **Not required** for an "agriculturist" (an individual cultivating by own, family or hired labour under personal supervision) **to the extent of supplying produce from cultivation of land** (CGST Act Sec 23(1)(b), Sec 2(7)).
  - Also not required if you supply only exempt goods (Sec 23(1)(a)).
  - **If you also trade taxable goods** (pots, planters, potting mix, fertiliser packs, décor): register once aggregate turnover crosses **₹20 lakh, which is Telangana's goods threshold** (Telangana did not adopt the ₹40 lakh option), or register voluntarily.
  - Exempt supplies can count in "aggregate turnover", so get a CA's view.
- **Inputs after the GST 2.0 rationalisation (56th GST Council, 3 Sep 2025; effective 22 Sep 2025) (⚠️):**
  - chemical fertilisers 5% (unchanged);
  - fertiliser feedstocks (sulphuric acid, nitric acid, ammonia) cut from 18% to 5%;
  - micronutrients 12% → 5%;
  - **bio-pesticides 12% → 5%**;
  - **chemical pesticides 18%** (unchanged);
  - **drip and sprinkler systems 12% → 5%**;
  - **tractors (≤1800 cc) and power tillers and other soil-preparation/cultivation machinery 12% → 5%**;
  - tractor tyres and parts 18% → 5%;
  - **plastic pots and trays generally 18%** (HSN 3923/3924/3926 depending on product);
  - **UV-stabilised greenhouse film 18%** (3920);
  - **cocopeat/coir pith about 5%**;
  - **shade net 5% (if classed as fabric) or 18% (if classed as a plastic article)**;
  - GI pipe structures 18%.
  - Confirm each with your supplier's HSN code on https://cbic-gst.gov.in / https://gstcouncil.gov.in.
- **Effect on costs:** you **cannot claim input tax credit**, because your outputs are exempt. Treat GST on inputs as part of their cost.
- **Services (⚠️):** many farm services are GST-exempt, e.g., hiring farm labour, renting farm machinery for cultivation, and leasing land for agriculture.

## 6) Incentives, subsidies and government credit

> ⚠️ The cost norms below are from the **MIDH Operational Guidelines (2014)**, the last version known to the researcher, and from NHB, AIF and KCC rules as last known. **Not re-verified in this session.** Telangana may use revised norms, so get the current rate sheet from the District Horticulture Officer.

**MIDH (Mission for Integrated Development of Horticulture)**
- **Who runs it:** the Telangana Horticulture Dept. Central:state funding is 60:40. Apply to the **District Horticulture & Sericulture Officer / divisional Horticulture Officer**, and get **sanction before you build**. Use empanelled or approved vendors and design. (https://horticulture.tg.nic.in ✅ domain; https://midh.gov.in)
- **Protected cultivation (50% of cost norm, up to 4,000 m² per beneficiary):**
  - **Shade-net house (tubular): ₹710/m²** (wooden ₹492, bamboo ₹360). Subsidy about ₹355/m², or up to about ₹14.2 lakh at 4,000 m².
  - **Naturally ventilated polyhouse (tubular): ₹1,060/m² (≤500 m²), ₹935 (>500–1,008), ₹890 (>1,008–2,080), ₹844 (>2,080–4,000).** At 4,000 m² the cost norm is about ₹33.8 lakh and the subsidy about ₹16.9 lakh.
  - Fan-and-pad greenhouse: ₹1,650 down to ₹1,400/m².
  - Planting material and cultivation in polyhouse or shade net (50%): high-value vegetables ₹140/m²; orchids/anthurium ₹700; carnation/gerbera ₹610; rose/lilium ₹426.
  - **Plastic mulching:** ₹32,000/ha, 50%, up to 2 ha.
  - Pack-house (about 9 m × 6 m): ₹4 lakh unit cost, 50%.
- **Nurseries (planting material):**
  - **Small nursery (1 ha): cost norm ₹15 lakh/ha.** Private sector gets 50% as a credit-linked back-ended subsidy (max about ₹7.5 lakh).
  - **Hi-tech nursery (up to 4 ha): ₹25 lakh/ha.** Private sector gets 40% (max about ₹40 lakh).
  - These target **certified planting material of mandated fruit, plantation and spice crops**, with minimum annual output and accreditation. An **indoor ornamental nursery is unlikely to qualify**; a **dragon-fruit or fruit nursery might**.
- **Dragon fruit (area expansion):** MIDH and the state have promoted dragon fruit under fruit area expansion (typically 40% of cost norm in instalments). **The current Telangana rate was not verified**; ask the Horticulture Officer.
- **Earlier state top-up:** Telangana's former state-plan **polyhouse scheme (75% subsidy, 2014–18)** has a page on the department site (https://horticulture.tg.nic.in/PolyHouse/PolyHouse.html ✅ exists). **Whether any state top-up is active in 2025-26/2026-27 was not verified.**

**Micro-irrigation (PMKSY – Per Drop More Crop)**
- **Central rate:** **55% for small and marginal farmers, 45% for others**, on the indicative unit cost.
- **Telangana top-up:** Telangana historically topped up (SC/ST 100%, BC and small/marginal 90%, others 80%, through the Telangana State Micro Irrigation Project). **Current state top-up unconfirmed.**
- **How to apply:** through the Horticulture/Agriculture office with a registered MI company. The passbook is needed. (https://pmksy.gov.in ; https://horticulture.tg.nic.in)

**NHB "Development of Commercial Horticulture through Production & Post-Harvest Management"**
- **Subsidy:**
  - **open-field commercial projects:** 40% of project cost, max ₹30 lakh per project (general areas);
  - **protected cultivation (more than 2,500 m²):** 50% of project cost, max ₹56 lakh per project (project-cost ceiling about ₹112 lakh).
- **Conditions:** **credit-linked, back-ended.** You need a bank or FI term loan; the subsidy goes into the loan account after completion and a joint inspection, with a lock-in period.
- **Who and how:** individuals, firms and FPOs can apply. Apply online and get in-principle approval **before** starting.
- **Fit for you:** better suited to projects over about ₹20 lakh. A small shade-net house fits MIDH better. (https://nhb.gov.in)

**Agriculture Infrastructure Fund (AIF)**
- **Terms:**
  - **3% p.a. interest subvention** on loans up to **₹2 crore**, for up to **7 years**;
  - credit guarantee (CGTMSE) for loans up to ₹2 crore, with the fee paid by the government;
  - lenders' interest is capped.
- **Eligible borrowers** include **individual farmers**, agri-entrepreneurs, FPOs and start-ups.
- **Eligible projects:**
  - post-harvest projects (pack-houses, sorting/grading, cold storage/cold rooms, ripening chambers, primary processing);
  - "community farming assets", including smart and precision agriculture.
  - Polyhouse, greenhouse and hydroponic projects have been accepted under precision agriculture. **Confirm eligibility with the lender before applying.**
- **Combining:** AIF can usually be combined with MIDH or NHB capital subsidies. (https://agriinfra.dac.gov.in)

**Kisan Credit Card (KCC)**
- **Interest:** short-term crop loans at **7%**. The government pays 1.5% interest subvention to the bank, and you get a **3% prompt-repayment incentive**, so the effective rate is **4%**, on loans up to **₹3 lakh**.
- **₹5 lakh limit:** **Union Budget 2025-26 announced raising the Modified Interest Subvention Scheme limit to ₹5 lakh.** Implementation for 2025-26/2026-27 was not confirmed; ask your bank.
- **Collateral:** collateral-free agri loans up to **₹2 lakh** (RBI, effective 1 Jan 2025).
- **Eligibility:** a **new landowner can get a KCC once the land is mutated** (passbook) and a crop plan is given. Limits follow the district "scale of finance" for the crop. Polyhouse and nursery capital costs need a separate **term loan**. (https://agriwelfare.gov.in ; https://www.rbi.org.in)

**Other schemes**
- **PM Mudra Yojana:** loans up to ₹20 lakh (Tarun Plus from Oct 2024) for income-generating micro enterprises, including **activities allied to agriculture** and **trading/retail**.
  - A **plant-retail or trading** unit fits.
  - Pure crop cultivation is meant for KCC or agri term loans instead; banks differ on nurseries, so ask. (https://www.mudra.org.in)
- **PMEGP:** the negative list excludes businesses connected with **horticulture and floriculture** (and crop cultivation), so a plant nursery is **not eligible**. (https://www.kviconline.gov.in/pmegp/)
- **Agri-Clinics & Agri-Business Centres (ACABC):** 36% subsidy (44% for women, SC/ST and NE/hill areas) on projects up to about ₹20 lakh (individual). Only for **graduates or diploma holders in agriculture and allied subjects** (some related-science graduates also qualify). **Likely not applicable** unless you hold such a qualification. (https://www.agriclinics.net)
- **Telangana start-up or agri-tech schemes for an individual farmer-entrepreneur:** none identified (gap).

## c) Compliance checklist for starting (in order)

1. **Shortlist land, then check it.**
   - Pull the **Bhu Bharati** record: pattadar, survey number, extent, land type.
   - Get the **encumbrance** history.
   - Check the **22-A prohibited list**.
   - Rule out **assigned, inam, Bhoodan, ceiling-surplus, Wakf, endowment, forest and government land**.
   - Rule out any **Scheduled (Agency) Area** village.
   - Check road access, power lines and water.
   - Get a **survey/demarcation** and a **lawyer's title opinion**.
2. **Buy and register.**
   - Book a slot on Bhu Bharati; registration is by the Tahsildar.
   - Make sure **mutation** follows and collect the **pattadar passbook** (and Bhudhaar ID where issued).
   - Ownership disputes go to RDO/Collector, then the Land Tribunal (CCLA meanwhile).
3. **Settle land use before building.**
   - Shade-net, polyhouse and drip count as agricultural use; confirm in writing with the Tahsildar/Gram Panchayat that no NALA or building permission is needed.
   - A farmhouse, worker quarters, office or shop needs **NALA conversion** plus **building permission**.
4. **Power connection.**
   - Apply to TGSPDCL (or the new rural discom) with the passbook.
   - Decide the category: **LT-V(A) free** for a field pump; **LT-V(B) ₹4/unit** for a horticulture nursery.
5. **Plan subsidies before construction.**
   - Get a project estimate.
   - Apply to the **District Horticulture Officer (MIDH)** for shade-net/polyhouse, mulch and planting material, and for **micro-irrigation (PDMC)**.
   - For larger projects, arrange a **bank term loan** and apply for **NHB** in-principle approval and/or **AIF** interest subvention.
   - **Wait for the sanction/work order before you build.**
6. **Licences.**
   - **Nursery licence** from the Horticulture Officer (₹1,000/₹2,500, valid 3 years).
   - **Seed-dealer licence** from the Agriculture Dept if you will sell vegetable seedlings or seeds.
   - **Shops & Establishments** registration if you open a city shop or office.
7. **Sourcing plants.**
   - Buy from licensed nurseries or tissue-culture labs with bills.
   - For imports: **PQ import permit (PQ 02)**, **phytosanitary certificate**, and post-entry quarantine where specified.
   - **CITES permits** for CITES species.
   - **No Schedule III specified plants** without a Chief Wildlife Warden licence.
   - Avoid propagating **PPV-protected varieties** for sale without the breeder's licence.
8. **Farm inputs.** Use only **CIB&RC-registered** pesticides with label claims; keep dealer bills. No under-18s handling pesticides.
9. **Hire workers properly.**
   - Written appointment letters.
   - At least **₹538/day (₹14,000/month) for rural unskilled** workers, more for skilled.
   - Wages paid on time (by the 7th for monthly staff; end of shift for daily); attendance and wage register; weekly rest day; overtime at twice the rate; equal pay.
   - **Employees' compensation insurance.**
   - Help workers register on **e-Shram**.
   - At **10** workers: ESIC, gratuity, maternity, POSH, OSH registration, and migrant-worker rules (if 10+ migrants). At **20**: EPF and bonus.
10. **Farmer schemes.**
    - Open a farm bank account and apply for a **KCC**.
    - Get your crop booked by the **AEO** each season so you can claim **Rythu Bharosa** (₹6,000/acre/season).
    - Note that **PM-KISAN** (cut-off), **Rythu Bima** (lapsed) and **PMFBY** (not running) are not available. Buy private structure, crop and term-life cover.
11. **Tax and accounts.**
    - Keep separate books for **own-grown** produce (agricultural income) and **bought-for-resale** plants or accessories (business income).
    - File **ITR-2/ITR-3** reporting agricultural income.
    - GST registration is not needed for own produce. Register if taxable trading exceeds **₹20 lakh**.
12. **Selling.**
    - Vegetables and fruit: Rythu Bazars (farmer ID), market yards (buyer pays the fee), or direct to retailers and restaurants.
    - Plants: direct, online, or through your own retail outlet (Shops & Establishments registration).
